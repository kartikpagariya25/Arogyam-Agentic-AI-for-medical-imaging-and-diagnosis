Radiological imaging modalities include X-ray, CT, MRI, and Ultrasound.

X-ray:
- Best for bones and chest pathology.
- Radiolucent areas appear black (air), radiopaque areas white (bone).
- Common findings: consolidation, pneumothorax, fractures, effusions.

CT Scan:
- Cross-sectional imaging with high spatial resolution.
- Useful for trauma, lung, and brain evaluation.
- Uses Hounsfield units to describe tissue density.

MRI:
- Superior for soft tissue contrast.
- T1-weighted: fat is bright, water is dark.
- T2-weighted: water and pathology appear bright.
- Used for CNS, musculoskeletal, and liver lesions.

Ultrasound:
- Safe, real-time imaging using sound waves.
- Anechoic (black) = fluid; hyperechoic (white) = dense tissue.
- Doppler helps assess blood flow.

Contrast Studies:
- Use iodine (CT) or gadolinium (MRI) to enhance vascular and tissue visualization.
- Always check renal function before contrast administration.

Artifacts and Limitations:
- Motion blur, metal implants, and operator dependency affect quality.
- Correct positioning and exposure are essential for diagnosis accuracy.
