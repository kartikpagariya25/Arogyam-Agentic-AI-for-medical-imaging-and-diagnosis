Skin diseases are broadly classified into inflammatory, infectious, and neoplastic conditions.

Psoriasis:
- Chronic immune-mediated disorder with sharply demarcated erythematous plaques and silvery scale.
- Common sites: scalp, elbows, knees, lower back.
- Auspitz sign: pinpoint bleeding when scales are scraped off.
- Koebner phenomenon: lesions appear at sites of trauma.
- Histology shows parakeratosis, elongation of rete ridges, Munro microabscesses.

Eczema (Dermatitis):
- Characterized by pruritus, erythema, vesiculation, and scaling.
- Can be atopic, contact, seborrheic, or nummular.
- Chronic forms show lichenification (thickened skin from scratching).

Fungal Infections (Tinea):
- Caused by dermatophytes such as Trichophyton and Microsporum.
- Annular lesions with central clearing and active scaly borders.
- Diagnosed with KOH mount or culture on Sabouraud agar.

Seborrheic Dermatitis:
- Greasy, yellow scales on scalp, nasolabial folds, eyebrows.
- Associated with Malassezia yeast overgrowth.
- Responds to antifungal shampoos and mild corticosteroids.

Vitiligo:
- Acquired depigmenting disorder due to loss of melanocytes.
- Sharply defined white macules, often symmetric.
- Common on face, hands, and genital areas.

General note:
Lesion morphology, distribution, and surface change are critical in differentiating these conditions.
